ICT Sectors & Careers: "The people behind ICT"

Programmers

Creates and maintains computer programs and software using programming languages.

Web Developer

Design, builds, and maintains websites and web applications.

Graphic Designer

Creates visual designs such as posters, logos, advertisements, and digital graphics.

Animator

Creates moving images or visual effects for films, games, advertisements, and other media.

IT Support Specialist

Helps users troubleshoot and solve computer, network, and technical problems.

Cybersecurity

Protects computers, networks, systems, and data from cyberattacks and security threats.

Data Analysts

They help organizations organize, manage, and understand data so they can make better decisions.

E-Commerce

Is the process of buying and selling goods and services over the internet through various digital devices and platforms.

Digital Citizenship

Using technology responsibly, respectfully, safely, and appropriately.

Netiquette (Network + Etiquette)

The "PROPER" manner in an online community environment.

Core Rules of Online Courtesy

1. Remember the Human

There is a real person behind the screen.

2. Follow Real-Life Standards

The standards of honesty, respect, responsibility, and appropriate behavior that we follow in real life should also apply online.

3. Know Where You Are

Different online spaces have different expectations. Know the audience, purpose, rules, and culture of the online space.

4. Make Yourself Look Good

Your online behavior says something about you.

5. Share Expert Knowledge

If you know something useful, share it.

6. Respect Privacy

Not everything you see is yours to share.

7. Don't Abuse Your Power

Just because you CAN doesn't mean you SHOULD.

8. Forgive Mistakes

Nobody is perfect online.

Types of Cyberbullying

Harassment

Repeatedly sending unwanted, hurtful, threatening, or abusive messages.

Denigration

Spreading false, cruel, or damaging information about someone online.

Outing

Sharing someone's private or embarrassing information without permission.

Impersonation

Pretending to be another person online, especially to cause harm or embarrassment.

Exclusion

Deliberately leaving someone out of an online group or activity in a way intended to hurt or humiliate them.

Cyberstalking

Repeatedly monitoring, contacting, threatening, or intimidating someone through digital means.

Digital Footprint

The trail of information and activity you leave when using digital technologies and the internet. (Post → Share → Screenshot → Save)

Examples of Digital Footprint

  • Social media posts: Content, updates, and photos uploaded to social networks.
  • Online platforms: Accounts, activity, and interactions across the web.
  • Reviews & ratings: Feedback left on products, services, or locations.
  • Messaging: Chats, direct messages, and communication histories.
  • Cookies & trackers: Data collected by websites to track browsing habits.
  • IP addresses & metadata: Technical data revealing location and device details.
  • App data: Information stored or shared while using mobile and desktop applications.

How Do We Protect Ourselves?

Online safety, security and ethical practices are crucial for protecting yourself and others in the digital world.
